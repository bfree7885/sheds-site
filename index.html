Sheds

Terrain-based shed hunting navigation and field intelligence.

Sheds is a project developed by Waypoint Studio. The goal is to help people explore landscapes responsibly while searching for naturally shed deer antlers. The platform combines mapping, terrain analysis, and simple field logging so users can better understand how deer use different parts of the landscape.

Field Navigation

Sheds is designed to become one of the most reliable and easy-to-use navigation tools for shed hunters. The system focuses on terrain awareness, simple field use, and reliable performance in remote areas.

  • Terrain-based map layers
  • GPS position tracking
  • Waypoint and observation logging
  • Parking and access point marking
  • Route tracking for exploration
  • Offline map capability for remote areas

Many shed hunting locations have little or no cell service. Sheds is designed to work both online and offline so users can continue navigating and recording observations anywhere.

Simple Field Logging

Field logging should be quick and easy. Recording a shed or observation should take only a few seconds.

When a user logs a find, the system can automatically record environmental context such as location, terrain slope, elevation, habitat type, and distance to water or agricultural edges.

Users may also upload photos and optional notes from the field. Future tools may help estimate antler characteristics such as point count, main beam length, age class, and shed condition.

Building a Landscape Dataset

Each observation recorded in Sheds contributes to a growing geospatial dataset that describes where sheds occur across different landscapes and terrain conditions.

Over time this information can be used to improve terrain-based probability models that generate shed hunting heatmaps inside the platform.

As more observations are collected, the heatmaps become more accurate by learning from real locations where sheds have been found.

All information is recorded and stored using consistent data standards so it can support professional analysis.

Aggregated and anonymized datasets may eventually be made available to universities, researchers, conservation organizations, or land managers studying wildlife movement, habitat use, and landscape ecology.
