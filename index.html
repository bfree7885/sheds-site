Sheds

GIS-based shed hunting decision support and field navigation.

Sheds is a terrain-focused mapping and observation platform designed to help people explore landscapes responsibly while searching for naturally shed deer antlers.

The project combines mapping, terrain analysis, and simple field logging tools to help users understand how wildlife interacts with landscapes. Rather than encouraging pressure on animals, Sheds emphasizes awareness, seasonal timing, and thoughtful exploration.

Over time, observations recorded through the platform may contribute to a large-scale geospatial dataset describing where shed antlers appear across different terrain types and environmental conditions.

Field Navigation

Sheds is designed to function as a reliable field navigation tool for shed hunters, hikers, and wildlife observers.

  • Terrain-based map layers
  • Offline map capability for remote areas
  • GPS position tracking
  • Waypoint and observation logging
  • Parking and access point marking
  • Route tracking for field exploration

Many shed hunting locations have limited or no cell service. Sheds is designed to function both online and offline so users can navigate and record observations anywhere.

Simple Field Logging

Logging a shed or observation should take only a few seconds.

When a find is recorded, the system can automatically attach environmental context such as terrain characteristics, slope direction, elevation, and proximity to water or agricultural edges.

Users can also upload photos and optional notes about the find. Future tools may help estimate antler characteristics such as point count, main beam length, age class, and condition.

Building a Landscape Dataset

Sheds is designed to collect structured environmental information related to shed antler locations.

Over time this creates a geospatial dataset describing how terrain, habitat, and seasonal conditions influence where antlers are shed.

Aggregated and anonymized versions of this dataset may eventually support academic research, wildlife ecology studies, and landscape analysis by universities and conservation organizations.

What Sheds Will Provide

Sheds is designed to reduce guesswork around where and when it is appropriate to spend time in the field. Rather than focusing on finds or outcomes, it focuses on context—seasonal timing, land status, terrain features, and environmental conditions that influence wildlife behavior.

Over time, Sheds will combine multiple spatial signals into a single, interpretable view so users can make calmer, more informed decisions before heading out. The goal is not to direct movement, but to support restraint, awareness, and intentional planning.

Access to advanced terrain decision-support layers will eventually be available through a subscription model. This allows Sheds to remain independent, avoid advertising pressure, and prioritize ethical constraints by design.

How Sheds Is Different

  • Focuses on when not to go as much as where to go
  • Encourages awareness rather than maximizing finds
  • Uses terrain and environmental context instead of hype
  • Supports research-quality environmental observations
  • Designed around ethical wildlife interaction
