Field Notes

Field Notes tracks current environmental conditions that influence shed hunting across North America.

Updates summarize winter severity, snowpack, wildlife agency reports, and research related to deer movement and antler drop timing.

Why Shed Hunting Feels Harder Than It Should

March 4, 2026

Late winter conditions across much of the whitetail range are creating compressed deer movement. Limited food availability, snow depth, and energy conservation often concentrate deer in smaller habitat zones.

Sheds Take: Focus on south-facing slopes and thermal bedding cover near winter food sources. Concentrated deer activity increases the probability of sheds appearing in these areas.

Winter Weather and Whitetail Pressure: What 2026 Conditions Suggest

March 4, 2026

Extended cold periods and fluctuating snow depth this winter are likely increasing metabolic stress in deer populations across several regions.

Sheds Take: During high winter stress periods, deer limit unnecessary movement. Search near bedding cover and terrain that provides wind protection and solar exposure.

Snowpack and Shed Distribution

March 3, 2026

Deep or persistent snowpack alters deer travel routes and feeding behavior. As snow begins to melt, sheds that dropped earlier in winter often become visible along compressed travel corridors.

Sheds Take: Watch melt patterns carefully. Sheds often appear along exposed south-facing slopes and feeding edges as snow recedes.
